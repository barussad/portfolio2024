Photography

Bubble Reflections

This series explores how unstable the perception of a person on a photo can be. Inspired by Laurence Demaison’s distorted self-portraits, I photographed my model’s reflections in soap bubbles and oil, using their curved, moving surfaces to break the face into shifting shapes.

Instead of perfect, flattering portraits, the images show faces that melt, double, or disappear inside the reflections. The bubbles and oil texture last only a moment, so each photograph is an outcome between control and chance.

Fisheye Photography

The concept behind this project was about meeting new people and overcoming my fear of stepping out of my comfort. Stopping “just looking through a peephole.” Instead of staying safely on the other side of the door, watching life from a distance, I used this project as a way to open that door a little wider each time and let real encounters happen.

With this work, I challenged myself to move closer and to start conversations, ask questions, and spend time with strangers I would usually just pass by.

Portraits

Portraits and closeups taken with the intention of using them as an album cover.

The images are shot with album layout in mind, leaving intentional negative space where titles or tracklists could sit. The round light is supposed to evoke the shape of a vinyl record.

Irish Landscape

A series of photographs taken around Sligo (Ireland), focusing on the specific Irish green colour, misty hills, and hidden waterfalls. I tried to capture the quiet, slightly wild atmosphere of the place: the soft light, moving water, and small details that usually disappear in passing.

I played with slower shutter speeds on the waterfalls to blur the water into soft lines, contrasting it with sharp rocks, trees, and moss. The colours stay natural, keeping the focus on light, texture, and the mood of the unusually nice Irish weather rather than on heavy editing.

Experimental Car Photography

I’m not particularly a fan of cars, yet I somehow keep ending up at car meets. Instead of trying to force myself to love the subject, I decided to make the photography itself the fun part. The project became less about “documenting cool cars” and more about playing with how they can be seen.

To keep it interesting, I experimented with different techniques: shooting with a fisheye lens to exaggerate curves and stretch perspectives or using a kaleidoscope to fragment the image into repeating patterns. This kind of creates David Hockney–style “joiners” by assembling multiple photos of the same car into one collage. These methods break away from the usual polished car shots and instead highlight distortion, texture, and rhythm.

Analogue Photography

35mm Photographs taken by a Pentax P30t camera on Illford HP5 Plus film and developed on an Illford Multigrain RC Deluxe paper.

Pinhole Photography

This project is an experiment in seeing the world without lenses or autofocus, by building a simple pinhole camera and used long exposures to let light slowly etch itself onto the photopaper. Instead of chasing sharpness and technical perfection, the aim was to embrace softness, vignetting, and slight distortions as part of the image.
